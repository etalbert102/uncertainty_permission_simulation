## 8. Limitations and Conclusion

### 8.1 Limitations

The formal results are conditional. Robust permission depends on the declared compatible-refinement set, independently owned support rule, fully instantiated action identities, and ordering that separates progression from constraint, information acquisition, and restoration. An omitted refinement or misspecified support rule can change an assessment. These dependencies limit the conclusion to systems where those objects can be specified and inspected. Missing evidence or rules should produce `unassessable`, not an inferred pass. New domains require their own evaluation specification and refinement-coverage argument.

The Robodebt application establishes documented structural relevance, not numerical calibration. The early and enhanced OCI profiles are bounded by the selected sources, dates, and recorded precision. Executable queue, engagement, retrieval, service, correction, and restoration values are stylized. Simulation rates therefore do not estimate historical Robodebt prevalence, capacity, delay, debt error, or causal effects. Historical quantification would require a separately sourced dataset, new measurement model, and new design freeze. The study supplies no monetary-entitlement oracle and makes no independent legal judgment.

Four measurement limits restrict comparison. First, the decision-service output is excluded because its numerator did not verify a supported disposition by the service deadline; repair requires a revised metric and affected rerun. Second, interaction events record responses and assistance requests, not evidence-production effort, psychological cost, welfare, or subjective burden. U3's additional interactions therefore leave substantive burden unresolved. Third, the frozen analysis emitted no registered paired correction or restoration estimand. The open-or-administratively-censored terminal-path rate was 1.000 in every U0, U2C, and U3 family cell; this combines structural non-absorption with work continuing to day 650. Earlier stage outcomes remain interpretable, but complete-resolution and complete-restoration comparisons do not. Fourth, MCSE measures finite-run precision only, while unweighted cross-family means imply no real-world scenario distribution.

The kernel represents declared rules, finite queues, deadlines, keyed exogenous variation, and modeled correction paths. It does not model reviewer psychology, discretion, deference to automated outputs, strategic adaptation, or endogenous organizational learning. Nor does it supply a scalar welfare function. Safety, timeliness, unresolved holding, recipient interactions, agency workload, exposure, correction, and restoration remain separate coordinates, so the results cannot identify a universally optimal policy. Choosing among them requires normative judgments and institutional evidence outside the simulation.

Finally, the protocol is an inspectable design artifact rather than an evaluated intervention. The study provides executable examples and a cross-vocabulary canary, but no practitioner study, usability validation, adoption evidence, or estimate of inter-rater agreement. The canary supports bounded representability in a contemporary AI-style workflow; it does not establish field transfer or prevalence. Process susceptibility also does not prove historical attackability: inducibility, observability, repeatability, and actor benefit were not established. Those claims require new empirical evidence rather than stronger language about the present artifacts.

### 8.2 Conclusion

The central question is what the workflow may newly do when decision-relevant evidence is lost. Under the declared refinement and support rules, weaker evidence cannot justify automatic progression unless the action remains independently supported across the admissible refinements. It may permit appropriately evaluated constraint, information, or restoration actions without expanding automatic progression. This locates authority in the evidence-to-action process rather than in a model output or formal policy label alone.

The simulation shows why that distinction becomes operational under load. Across three loads, fail-closed review preserved gate safety while timely review completion declined and unresolved holding increased. With review machinery and paired cases fixed, fail-open timeouts converted non-completion into unsupported progression and recovery exposure. The information-first policy likewise prevented unsupported progression but added interaction and agency-workload events and increased modeled exposure relative to conservative holding; exposure is a process state, not established error or harm. Its effect on substantive recipient burden remains unknown. These are designed-parameter-space results, not a historical capacity threshold or Robodebt reconstruction.

The study supports no single policy ranking. Nominal review, notice, or appeal cannot by themselves establish meaningful control. Control depends on whether uncertainty remains visible, whether the gate binds the exact action, whether an empowered reviewer can act before timeout, what follows non-completion, and whether correction restores every modeled consequence. The invalid service measure blocks a timely-supported-service comparison, while terminal censoring blocks complete-restoration comparisons for the most heavily censored policies.

For process red teaming, the transferable diagnostic is compact: when evidence becomes weaker, what may the system now do; who can still stop it; and can they do so before the consequence becomes difficult to restore? Answering those questions requires tracing evidence through representation, permission, capacity, action, and restoration. That full chain—not the presence of a reviewer or the local correctness of one component—is the relevant unit of governance.
